Groom the whole backlog in {path}. Groom every Issue there as you would groom one: make it precise enough to plan (what is wanted, what is out of scope, and how anyone will know it is done), with the effort fitted to its size. For a bug report, say how to reproduce it. In each one's front matter, set `difficulty:` to one of easy, medium, hard or developer. Use developer when the result needs the developer to check it by hand before it goes to main. Use hard when it is too big to do in one piece; in that case, split it by writing each part as a new file in issues/backlog/, named for the part, with `difficulty:` and `parent:` naming the Issue split in its front matter, and `waits_on:` listing any part that must land first. If an Issue cannot be done as written, add a `# Needs elaboration` section saying what is missing: the Issue then sits out until someone answers it, and the pass carries on. You may remove a `Needs elaboration` section you have answered.

Then rank the backlog in issues/backlog/ORDER, one slug per line, judged across the whole backlog: what should be done first goes first. The lines above the `# groomed below` line are the developer's placements; leave them exactly as they are. Below that line, name every backlog Issue the developer has not placed, once each, and nothing else. If ORDER or its `# groomed below` line is missing, add it after any lines already there.

Do not delete a backlog Issue or move one out of issues/backlog/, and do not touch issues/roadmap/ or anything outside issues/.
